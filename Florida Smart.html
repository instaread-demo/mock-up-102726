Florida Weddings: The Complete Guide to Getting Married in Florida

From barefoot beach ceremonies and intimate elopements to elegant resorts, historic properties, lush gardens, waterfront venues, and beautiful state parks, Florida offers almost every kind of wedding setting you can imagine. Whether you’re a Florida couple planning a local wedding or traveling from another state for a destination wedding, this guide will help you choose where to get married, understand the legal requirements, find the right venue, and plan a wedding that fits your style, budget, and vision.

Florida Wedding Regions

Florida is a large state, and each region offers a different wedding experience. The Atlantic Coast, Gulf Coast, Florida Keys, Central Florida, and Panhandle all have their own scenery, venues, weather patterns, and personality. Explore our regional wedding venue guides for inspiration, from Northeast Florida and the Space Coast to Central Florida, Tampa Bay, Southwest Florida, Southeast Florida, the Florida Keys, and Northwest Florida.

Florida Wedding Venues

Florida wedding venues range from beachfront resorts and historic properties to gardens, private estates, restaurants, barns, museums, country clubs, waterfront venues, and unique outdoor spaces. When choosing a venue, consider your guest count, ceremony and reception locations, catering options, parking, accessibility, vendor restrictions, alcohol policies, and what happens if the weather doesn’t cooperate. Explore our Florida Wedding Venues Guide →

Florida Outdoor Weddings

Outdoor weddings are one of the most popular ways to take advantage of Florida’s natural beauty. Gardens, waterfront lawns, private estates, historic properties, farms, parks, and other outdoor locations can create a beautiful setting, but you’ll also need to consider heat, humidity, rain, wind, insects, shade, guest comfort, restrooms, electricity, and your backup plan. Read our Florida Outdoor Weddings Guide →

Florida Beach Weddings

Florida’s beaches provide some of the most memorable wedding settings in the state, whether you’re planning a simple barefoot ceremony or a full destination wedding. Beach rules vary depending on whether the beach is managed by a city, county, state park, resort, or private property owner, so always check requirements for permits, decorations, chairs, music, pets, photography, parking, and other restrictions before booking your ceremony location. Read our Florida Beach Weddings Guide →

Florida State Park Weddings

Florida’s state parks offer beautiful and often affordable wedding settings that can include beaches, forests, lakes, gardens, historic buildings, pavilions, and scenic overlooks. Weddings and other special events are permitted at Florida State Parks, but requirements vary by individual park, so couples should contact the park directly to find out about availability, permits, fees, designated wedding areas, and event restrictions.

Florida Elopements

Florida is an excellent destination for couples who want an intimate wedding without the expense and logistics of a large celebration. A Florida elopement can be as simple as exchanging vows on the beach or as elaborate as an intimate ceremony at a private estate, resort, garden, historic property, or state park followed by dinner with your closest family and friends. Explore the Best Places to Elope in Florida →

Florida Marriage License

Before your Florida wedding can become official, you’ll need a Florida marriage license. Marriage licenses are issued through Florida’s Clerk of the Circuit Court offices, and couples do not have to be Florida residents to get married in the state. Requirements, fees, office locations, and procedures should be confirmed with the appropriate county before your trip, especially if you’re planning a destination wedding. Read our Florida Marriage License Guide →

Florida Wedding Permits

Your marriage license gives you permission to legally marry, but it does not automatically give you permission to hold your ceremony at a public location. Beaches, parks, state parks, historic properties, and other public spaces may require a separate permit or reservation, and requirements can vary based on the number of guests, decorations, chairs, vendors, music, alcohol, and other details. Always check with the organization that manages your chosen location before making plans. Learn more in our Florida Marriage License Guide →

Florida Wedding Weather

Florida wedding weather varies considerably by region and season, so your wedding date can have a major impact on your experience. Winter and spring are popular for outdoor weddings because of generally more comfortable temperatures, while summer brings heat, humidity, and frequent afternoon storms, and hurricane-season considerations become important later in the year. Whatever season you choose, an outdoor wedding should include a realistic weather backup plan.

Florida Wedding Regions

Northeast Florida: From Jacksonville and St. Augustine to Amelia Island and Ponte Vedra Beach, Northeast Florida combines historic charm, Atlantic beaches, waterfront locations, and beautiful outdoor venues. Explore Northeast Florida Wedding Venues →

East Central Florida: The Space Coast and surrounding Atlantic communities offer beaches, waterfront venues, gardens, resorts, and unique wedding locations from Daytona Beach and New Smyrna Beach to Cocoa Beach and Melbourne. Explore East Central Florida Wedding Venues →

Central Florida: Central Florida offers far more than theme parks, with gardens, historic properties, lakeside venues, resorts, private estates, and unique wedding locations throughout the Orlando area and surrounding communities. Explore Central Florida Wedding Venues →

Central West Florida: The Tampa Bay area and surrounding Gulf Coast communities offer waterfront venues, beaches, gardens, historic properties, resorts, and other distinctive locations for couples planning everything from intimate ceremonies to large celebrations. Explore Central West Florida Wedding Venues →

Southwest Florida: Southwest Florida is known for beautiful Gulf beaches, tropical landscapes, spectacular sunsets, barrier islands, and resort communities, making it a favorite for couples who want a relaxed coastal wedding. Explore Southwest Florida Wedding Venues →

Southeast Florida: Southeast Florida offers a more sophisticated coastal atmosphere, with luxury resorts, waterfront venues, tropical gardens, glamorous beaches, and destinations such as Palm Beach, Fort Lauderdale, Miami, and the surrounding communities. Explore Southeast Florida Wedding Venues →

Florida Keys: The Florida Keys provide a distinctly tropical destination wedding experience, with turquoise water, waterfront resorts, intimate venues, spectacular sunsets, and an island atmosphere that makes the wedding feel like a vacation for everyone involved. Explore Florida Keys Wedding Venues →

Northwest Florida: Northwest Florida is home to some of the state’s most beautiful white-sand beaches and emerald Gulf waters, with wedding destinations including Destin, Pensacola, Panama City Beach, Santa Rosa Beach, Fort Walton Beach, and the surrounding communities. Explore Northwest Florida Wedding Venues →

Planning a Florida Destination Wedding

Florida is especially well suited to destination weddings because your guests can turn the celebration into a vacation. Consider choosing a location where guests can stay nearby, enjoy beaches and attractions, and easily travel to the ceremony and reception. Giving guests plenty of advance notice can also make it easier for them to arrange transportation and accommodations, particularly in popular destinations and during peak wedding seasons.

Florida Weddings on a Budget

A Florida wedding doesn’t have to be expensive. Couples can keep costs manageable by choosing an intimate guest list, considering weekday or off-season dates, selecting a state park or other affordable location, choosing a brunch or dinner reception instead of a traditional full-day celebration, or planning a simple elopement. The key is deciding which parts of the wedding matter most to you and putting your budget toward those priorities.

DIY Wedding, Venue, or Wedding Planner?

There is no single right way to plan a Florida wedding. A DIY wedding can give you flexibility and control, a traditional venue can simplify many of the logistics, and a wedding planner can be especially valuable for destination weddings, large celebrations, or complicated outdoor events. Think about how much planning you enjoy, how much time you have, your guest count, and how much responsibility you want to handle yourself.

 

Ready to Plan Your Florida Wedding?

Whether you’re dreaming of a quiet ceremony for two, a barefoot beach wedding, an elegant resort celebration, a garden ceremony, or a large destination wedding surrounded by family and friends, Florida gives you plenty of ways to make it your own. Start by choosing the type of wedding and region that fit your vision, then use our guides to find the location, venue, legal information, and planning resources you need to turn that vision into your Florida wedding.
