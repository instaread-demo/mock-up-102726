Xi Jinping launches Communist party plenum to deepen anti-corruption and self-governance in China

Elite Central Committee members convene in Beijing for a closed-door session focused on strict party discipline and key anti-corruption measures

Chinese President Xi Jinping has opened the ruling Communist Party’s fifth plenary session, spearheading a high-stakes closed-door meeting focused on strict party governance, institutional discipline, and ideological loyalty.

Communist Party began a four-day meeting of its highest-ranking members on Monday, October 26, targeting concrete measures for "full and rigorous party self-governance", as President Xi Jinping seeks to reinforce his authority and enforce ideological uniformity.

According to state news agency Xinhua, Xi briefed the party's Central Committee on a draft document that said addressed the challenges the party faced, including growing its governing capacity, as well as guarding against complacency and combating corruption, the president's signature campaign.

The commentary wrote, the implementation of major decisions and plans of the Central Committee, the largest of its top decision-making bodies, must not be empty rhetoric.

"It is essential to both refine institutional rules and regulations to close loopholes and strengthen safeguards, and ensure their effective implementation," it said.

Analysts say this could be the last major party gathering before its 2027 leadership reshuffle, when Xi is expected to hold on to the top job for a fourth term.

Key takeaways & core developments from the fifth plenum agenda:

Approximately 300 members and alternate members of the 20th Central Committee convened in Beijing for the four-day closed-door gathering to deliberate on a draft policy decision advancing "full and rigorous Party self-governance".

As reported, the session emphasizes refined anti-corruption mechanisms, stricter oversight of public spending and major projects, and zero tolerance for administrative laxity or political factionalism within party ranks.

Following sweeping anti-corruption investigations across civilian ministries and military commands, the plenum provides a critical platform to evaluate senior personnel changes and reinforce centralized control.

What is a plenum?

Plenums are essentially closed door meetings of the Party's central committee that happen at least once a year.

There are usually seven plenums during each five-year Central Committee term and they are watched from afar because they are meant to signal the leadership's priorities.

The reform is known as the fifth plenum as reportedly being 5th since the current cohort was formed in 2022.

The timing for this year's gathering is important because it is a year away from the next Party Congress, where a new set of priorities is set up and a new central committee appointed.

Officials are expected to use the four-day meeting to ensure regional cadres strictly align with central policy priorities, maximizing administrative efficiency as Beijing addresses broader economic and strategic hurdles.

As informed, the Central Committee will convene in the closed-door meeting from Monday October 26 till Thursday October 29.
