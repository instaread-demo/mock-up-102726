PING i240 irons review

Written by Joe

Last Updated on April 14, 2026 by Matt

I've had a complicated relationship with PING irons. Not because they make bad clubs — they almost never do — but because I've historically found their offerings hard to get excited about. Functional, yes. Reliable, absolutely. But they've rarely made me feel anything at the range. I gamed a set of G425s a couple of years back and they were brilliant, in the way a dependable saloon car is brilliant: smooth, predictable, quietly excellent, and completely devoid of personality. So when the i240s arrived in July 2025, billed as the highest-launching players iron PING has ever made, I was curious but measured. I've been burned before by "higher launch" promises that translated to ballooning, low-spin fluff shots that pitched 40 feet past the flag. I wanted to see what these actually did for someone like me — a 13-handicap who hits enough fairways to be dangerous, but not enough greens to feel comfortable.

What Are They

The i240 irons are the 2025 replacement for the i230, one of the most respected players irons of the last few years — a set that Viktor Hovland trusted on tour. Big shoes to fill. PING is positioning the i240 as a players iron with the forgiveness properties closer to a game-improvement iron, and they're not being subtle about it. The key engineering story is the new multi-material cavity badge: a chrome-plated ABS and carbon fibre construction that comes in 8.5 grams lighter than the equivalent badge on the i230. That saved weight gets redistributed to lower the centre of gravity and increase perimeter weighting, particularly in the long and mid irons. Behind the face sits an activated elastomer insert in a toaster-style pocket, designed to improve energy transfer across the face and soften the feel at impact. PING also redesigned the grooves — fewer of them, but wider — at the direct request of their tour staff, who wanted better spin from the fairway. The head is cast 431 stainless steel and comes in standard, Power Spec (31.5-degree 7-iron), and Retro Spec (35-degree 7-iron) loft options. Standard 7-iron sits at 33 degrees. Price is $200 per club in steel, $232 in graphite, with stock shafts including the excellent Nippon NS Pro Modus3 Tour 115 and the PING Alta CB Blue for graphite players.

Look and Feel

At address, the i240 sits very cleanly. The topline is thin, the offset is progressive and subtle in the short irons, and the overall profile is compact enough that you don't feel like you're setting up behind a shovel. The carbon fibre badge visible from behind the club is divisive — several reviewers have noted it gives the iron a slightly busier look than the i230's cleaner aesthetic, and I'd agree. In hand, it reads slightly more "game improvement" than the i230 did, which may put off some better players on first impression. On the course, though, that concern evaporates quickly. The feel at impact is genuinely impressive for a cast iron — there's a soft, solid thud on pure strikes, and PING's sound tuning has clearly improved. Mishits don't sting, but they do tell you something went wrong. That distinction matters when you're trying to improve.

Distance and Ball Flight

The headline claim is high launch with a penetrating, controlled trajectory — not a ballooning pop-up. In my testing, that largely held true. With the standard 33-degree 7-iron, I was carrying the ball noticeably higher than with my current set, but critically, it wasn't uncontrollable. The ball launched up, held its line, and came down at a steep enough angle to stop on firm greens. From the rough, the wider grooves did seem to reduce the unpredictable flyer tendency I get with other irons — shots came out with more predictable spin and trajectory. Distance was consistent, without the artificial length you get from clubs with significantly jacked lofts. The loft is honest, which is something I appreciate when I'm trying to build reliable yardage numbers.

The spin rate is the number that stands out. At 7,100 rpm for a 13-handicapper, you're getting meaningful stopping power — these aren't low-spin distance irons in disguise. The 18.2-degree launch angle is higher than what I typically see from players irons in this category, which is exactly PING's intent. For a mid-handicapper, this means more greens held on firm courses, fewer times watching your approach skip through the back. The smash factor of 1.41 is solid and consistent across strikes, suggesting the face is doing its job on off-centre hits.

Forgiveness and Turf Interaction

PING claims an 11% reduction in dispersion compared to the i230. I can't verify that precisely on the course, but I can say that my shot pattern with the i240 was noticeably tighter than I expected from a club with a players iron profile. Off-centre hits stayed online better than any comparable iron I've tested at this price point. The sole is where PING has consistently been ahead of the field for years — the rounded leading edge and generous bounce made clean contact from tight lies and divot-heavy range mats alike. Turf interaction was smooth and forgiving without feeling like the club was skipping through the turf. One honest caveat: if you are a steep, digger-style striker, the bounce and leading edge may feel a touch resistant at first. It rewards a shallower, sweeping attack angle, which is something to be aware of if your swing has a more aggressive descent.

Who Are They Actually For

These are not beginner irons, and PING isn't pretending they are. But they're also not purely for scratch golfers with a consistent, repeatable ball strike. The i240 sits in a sweet spot for golfers in the 6-to-16 handicap range who are serious about improvement and want a club that will reward better swings while protecting them from the worst effects of a bad one. If you're coming from a chunky game-improvement iron and want to step into something that looks and feels more serious without abandoning all forgiveness, this is a compelling option. If you're already gaming a blade or a compact players iron and your consistency is good, you may not need what the i240 offers. For someone like me — a committed mid-handicapper who can stripe it but also can double-cross a 6-iron into trouble — the profile fits very well.

The Verdict

The PING i240 irons are the best argument PING has made in years for trusting them with your bag. The launch is genuinely high, the feel is better than a cast iron at this price has any right to be, and the forgiveness is real. The cavity badge aesthetic is the only thing that might give a vain golfer pause. At $200 per club, they're not cheap, but the performance justifies it. For a 13-handicap trying to break single figures, these are a serious, credible option.
