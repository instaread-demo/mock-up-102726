Best Thanksgiving Side Dishes (Make-Ahead Friendly)

The oven is the bottleneck on Thanksgiving, not the cooking. A turkey owns it for hours, and every side you planned is queued up behind it.

So the useful question is not which sides are best. It is which ones you can make on a quiet Sunday and still be proud of on Thursday.

Fourteen below, grouped by how far ahead they can be made, with what is left to do on the day. Two of them have to happen on the day and I have said so.

The make-ahead plan at a glance

Sides you can make weeks ahead and freeze

1. Stuffing

Stuffing takes the freezer better than anything else on the table.

Assemble it unbaked in the dish you plan to serve it in, wrap it twice, and bake it straight from frozen with the foil on until the middle is hot.

Drying the bread cubes is the part you can do weeks early. Our guide to the best bread for stuffing explains which loaves hold up, and freezing stuffing covers the wrapping.

2. Baked Mac And Cheese

Mac and cheese is the side that keeps children at the table, and it survives the freezer if you go heavy on the sauce.

Sauce always tightens as it sits, so make it looser than you want to eat it and hold back a little milk to whisk in on the day.

We have notes on freezing mac and cheese and on getting the sauce right if yours breaks.

3. Cornbread

Cornbread goes stale in a day and freezes for months, which makes baking it early the obvious move.

Cool it completely before it goes in the bag or the steam turns the crust to sponge.

See freezing cornbread for the wrapping, and reheating cornbread for getting the edges crisp again.

4. Dinner Rolls

Rolls out of the freezer beat rolls out of a bag, and nobody can tell the difference once they are warm.

Bake them a shade under, so the reheat finishes them instead of drying them out.

Our notes on freezing rolls cover the sweet ones, which dry out fastest.

5. Sweet Potato Casserole

The filling freezes cleanly. The topping does not, and that is the whole trick with this one.

Marshmallows go rubbery and pecans go soft, so leave both off until the casserole is already hot and give it ten minutes uncovered at the end.

Freezing sweet potato casserole has the timings.

Sides that are better made two or three days ahead

6. Cranberry Sauce

Cranberry sauce is the one side that is genuinely better for sitting. It sets properly and the raw edge comes off the fruit.

Make it the weekend before and forget about it.

If you are starting from dried fruit, this dried cranberry sauce works, and there are ways to make canned sauce taste homemade if the day gets away from you.

7. Turkey Gravy

Making gravy while everybody waits is the worst ten minutes of the day, and it is completely avoidable.

Roast a couple of turkey wings the week before, build the gravy from those, and stir in the real pan juices at the end so it still tastes like today’s bird.

Our turkey gravy guide covers the base.

8. Green Bean Casserole

Assembled early, this bakes straight from the fridge. Just keep the fried onions in the bag until the end.

Onions stirred in the night before turn to paste, which is the single most common complaint about this dish.

See reheating green bean casserole, or freeze it if you want it done even earlier.

9. Scalloped Potatoes

Sliced potatoes go gray in the fridge unless the cream covers them completely, so assemble the whole dish rather than prepping the potatoes alone.

Pull the foil for the last stretch or the top never browns.

The right cheese matters more than the recipe here, and yes, you can freeze them.

10. Broccoli Salad

Something cold and sharp cuts through a table of butter and cream, and this is the easiest version of that.

Keep the dressing in a jar and the broccoli, bacon and onion in a box, then combine a couple of hours out so it softens slightly without going limp.

A good store bought dressing is a fair shortcut, and freezing it works for the leftovers.

Sides to finish on the day, with the prep already done

11. Mashed Potatoes

Mashed potatoes made the day before are fine. Mashed potatoes reheated badly are wallpaper paste.

Hold them in a slow cooker on low with extra butter and a splash of warm cream, stirring now and then, and they come back better than they went in.

Season harder than feels right, because cold potatoes swallow salt. Our seasoning notes help, butter substitutes cover the dairy-free table, and here is how long they keep.

12. Glazed Carrots

Carrots are the side that always gets forgotten until the last twenty minutes, when there is no burner free.

Boil them until barely tender, cool them, and keep them in a box. On the day they need one pan, butter, brown sugar and ten minutes.

Softening carrots covers the timings, and cooked carrots freeze if you want them done even earlier.

13. Roasted Brussels Sprouts

Roast these early and you get khaki mush. This is one of the two things on the table that has to happen on the day.

What you can do ahead is all the knife work, plus rendering the bacon if you use it.

Hot oven, cut side down, no crowding. If you want the steakhouse version, our Longhorn copycat sprouts is the one people ask for.

14. Roasted Butternut Squash

Peeling squash is the whole job, and it keeps perfectly in a box for a few days once cubed.

It roasts in the residual heat while the turkey rests, or goes in the air fryer if every rack is full.

Air fryer butternut squash takes about fifteen minutes and frees the oven completely.

Reheating without ruining it

The rule that covers almost everything: lower temperature than you baked it, foil on, and a splash of liquid stirred in before it goes back in the oven. Around 325 is right for a casserole.

Crisp toppings go on at the end, never before. Fried onions, marshmallows, breadcrumbs and pecans all get their own ten minutes uncovered once the dish underneath is already hot.

Our guide to reheating a casserole goes through it dish by dish, and what goes with green beans is useful if you are still filling gaps in the menu.

Related questions

How far ahead can you make Thanksgiving side dishes?

Two to three days for anything that lives in the fridge, and up to three months for anything you freeze. Cranberry sauce, gravy and any assembled casserole are safe made ahead. The exceptions are the things you want crisp or bright green.

Which Thanksgiving sides freeze the best?

Stuffing, mac and cheese, cornbread, rolls and the filling of a sweet potato casserole. All of them are starch or sauce, which is what survives freezing. Salads, roasted vegetables and anything with a crunchy topping do not.

Can you make mashed potatoes the day before?

Yes, and they reheat well in a slow cooker with extra butter and warm cream stirred through. Make them slightly wetter and saltier than you want them, because they tighten and go flat in the fridge.

What is the best way to reheat sides without drying them out?

Covered, at a lower temperature than you baked them, with a splash of liquid added first. Around 325 with foil on gets a casserole hot without cooking it a second time.
