Parcheesi Board Game: Rules for How to Play

Parcheesi is based on the ancient board game Pachisi. In fact that are a lot of board games that can trace their origins to Pachisi. Pachisi differs enough that I won't go over the rules in this post. Pacheesi is similar enough to Ludo though, that you can use these rules to play Ludo as well. At the end of this post I will note where Ludo differs from Parcheesi.

Setup

  • Place the gameboard in the middle of the table.
  • Each player chooses a color and takes the four corresponding pawns. Place your pawns on the Start space that matches your chosen color. 
  • All of the players roll the dice. Some versions of the rules have you only rolling one of the dice. The player that rolls the highest starts the game. Play moves clockwise/left during the game. 

How to Play Parcheesi

To begin each of your turns you will roll both of the dice. Depending on what you roll, you will try to move your playing pieces around the gameboard. Pieces in Parcheesi move counter-clockwise around the gameboard. 

All of your pieces begin on your start space/circle. Before you can move your pieces, they must enter the gameboard. To enter the gameboard you must roll a five. You can either roll a five on one die, or the two dice can add up to five. When you roll a five, you must use it to move one of your pawns from the starting space to the enter space. You can move a pawn to the enter space even if you already have one of your pieces on the enter space. The only exception is you can't enter a piece if there are already two pieces on your enter space.

Should you not roll a five, you will move one of your pieces that are already on the board. The two numbers you rolled can be used separately or they can be used together. For example you roll a 4 and a 6. Your movement options include moving one piece four spaces and one piece six spaces, or moving one piece ten spaces. If you choose to use both dice for the same piece, you will use one of the dice at a time. For example with the 4 and 6 you will move the piece either four or six spaces first, and then move it the other number. 

Whenever possible you must move one or more of your pawns. You cannot decide not to move if there is a legal move available to you. If you can't use both dice to move but can use one of them, you must use the one die to move. Some versions of the rules say you have to use the higher die if you can.

You can only have two pieces of your color on the same space. This is called a blockade. If you already have two of your pieces on a space, you can't move another piece to the space or past it. See the Blockades section below for more details. 

Doublets

Rolling the same number on both of the dice is called a doublet. When you roll a doublet, you get to take additional actions on your turn. 

Should you roll the doublet when you still have pieces on your start space, you will use the numbers you rolled. You will then roll the dice again and use those dice to move your pieces. 

If you roll the doublet after you have taken all of your pieces out of your start space, you will get to take a special action. In this situation you will treat your roll as if you rolled the number on the top and bottom of each dice. The total spaces you will get to move will always add up to fourteen. For example you roll two fives. You will get to move your pieces as if you rolled two 5s and two 2s. You can use the four numbers in any combination. Below are just some of the combinations you can use.

  • Use all four numbers on one piece.
  • Use one number for all four pieces.
  • Move one pawn with three numbers and one piece with the other number.
  • Move two pawns with two of the numbers. 
  • Use two numbers for one pawn, and one number each for two other pawns. 

Should you be unable to use all four numbers, you don't get to use any of them. 

After you are done using the four numbers, you will roll the dice again. 

In some versions of the rules, if you are unable to use the full doublet you do not get to roll the dice again. 

Should you end up rolling doublets three times in a row, your turn ends immediately. You also must take your pawn that is closest to home, and return it to your start space. This rule is not present in the rules for all versions of the game.

Capturing Pawns

When moving around the gameboard you may land on a space that an opponent's pawn is on. If you use both dice to move the same pawn, this can be after you move with just the first die or both dice. When you land on another player's pawn, you capture it. The one exception is if the pawn is on a Safety space. See the Safety Space section below. 

You will return the captured pawn to it's corresponding start space. The corresponding player will have to re-enter it on the board by rolling a five on a future turn. 

After you capture a pawn, you can move one of your pawns 20 additional spaces at the end of your turn. If you can't move a pawn the full 20 spaces, you lose the bonus spaces. 

Should you capture the pawn with a doublet, you will finish off your current move. You will then move the 20 spaces, and then roll the dice again. 

Safety Spaces

Along the gameboard there are a number of Safety spaces. The appearance of the Safety spaces may differ based on the version of the game. 

When a pawn is on a Safety space, it cannot be captured. Two pawns of different colors can never share a space though. Therefore if your move would land you on an occupied Safety space, you cannot move your pawn to that space. You can place two of your pawns on the same Safety space though. 

Should an opponent's pawn be on the Safety space that corresponds to another player's enter space, that player can enter a pawn onto the space. This will capture the opponent's pawn that was on the space. You will return it to the player's Home space, and you will get the 20 bonus spaces at the end of your turn. 

Blockades

Whenever two pawns of the same color occupy a space, the player has created a blockade. A blockade is unique because it blocks all other pawns. No pawns can land on or move past a blockade. This includes pawns of the color matching the blockade. 

Should a blockade be formed on a player's enter space, the corresponding player cannot enter any pawns until the blockade is moved. 

If you cannot move any of your other pawns and you have a blockade, you must break the blockade by moving one or both of the pawns that were part of the blockade.

Should you roll a doublet, you cannot move both pawns from a blockade forward to recreate the blockade further along the gameboard. 

Some versions of the rules allow a player to break a blockade with a doublet roll of ones. 

Winning the Game

The goal of the game is to move your pawns counter-clockwise around the gameboard to reach the Home path. 

When you reach the entrance to your Home, you will move up the path. Once a pawn is on a player's Home path, it can no longer be captured by other players. You can only move onto your own Home path. You cannot move your pawn into another player's Home path. 

To move to the Home space at the end of the path, you must land on the space by exact count. The Home space counts as a space. If you roll a number that is too high, you can't move that pawn with the die roll. 

After you move a pawn to the Home space, you get 10 bonus spaces that you can use at the end of your turn. You must use all of the spaces on one pawn. If you can't use all 10 spaces, you don't get to use any of them. 

The first player to get all four of their pawns on the Home space, wins the game. 

Ludo

Here are the rules where Ludo differs from Parcheesi. 

The Ludo board has less spaces than a Parcheesi board. 

Ludo only uses one die, so the doublet rules are not used in the game. 

In Parcheesi you must roll a five to enter a piece onto the board. In Ludo you have to roll a six. You don't have to enter a piece each time you roll a six if you choose to move a piece with the six instead. 

If you roll a six and you don't have any pawns on your start space, you get to roll the die again and move with the die roll. Should you roll a six a third time, your turn immediately ends. 

If you capture another player's pawn, the pawn is sent back to its start space. You do not receive a bonus for capturing a pawn. 

Blockades are called blocks in Ludo. When another player's pawn lands on a block, it is returned to the player's start space. 

Components

  • Gameboard
  • 16 Pawns (4 of each color)
  • 2 Dice
  • Instructions
