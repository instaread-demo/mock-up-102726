In defence of pumpkin spice: It’s time to stop hating on fall’s favourite flavour

First off, let’s clear up the biggest misconception: There is no actual pumpkin in your latte. Now that we’ve got that out of the way, can we please let people enjoy their autumn in peace?

 

by Brianne Cail

Published: Monday 14th September 2026

  • Culture
Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by

Instaread

Before we begin, I need to clear the air on a massive, widespread rumour that seems to shock people anew every single September: Pumpkin spice does not actually contain pumpkin.

That’s right. Take a breath. Your beloved autumnal blend is not a purée of orange squash. It is, in fact, a cozy coalition of cinnamon, nutmeg, ginger, cloves and allspice — originally blended to season pumpkin pies, not to mimic the vegetable itself. So if your primary critique of fall’s most dominant food trend is "ew, squash in my coffee," you can officially retire that argument right now.

View on Instagram

What actually is pumpkin spice?

Before it became a viral beverage sensation, pumpkin spice was simply a humble, workhorse blend in the baker's pantry. Traditionally known as pumpkin pie spice, the mix dates back centuries to traditional spice trade routes when warm, aromatic spices like cinnamon, nutmeg, ginger, cloves and allspice were first combined to season baked squashes and autumnal pies.

It wasn't designed as a standalone flavouring for coffee or candles, although I eagerly await those hitting the shelves so I can grab some. It was an efficient, warm shortcut for home bakers looking to elevate rich, earthy fall harvests with a single pinch of spice.

The anti-basic backlash is tired

Somewhere around 2015, society collectively decided that enjoying a warm, aromatic blend of baking spices in a paper cup made a person "basic." We turned a delicious, centuries-old spice profile into a cultural punchline, treating anyone ordering an autumn-themed beverage like they were auditioning for a viral stereotype.

But honestly? The hate is exhausting. We live in a city where winter lasts roughly seven months, transit is a daily test of endurance and rent requires a small loan. If a dash of nutmeg and steamed milk brings someone a fleeting moment of pure, unadulterated joy on a rainy Tuesday morning on the TTC, who are we to judge?

View on Instagram

The culinary genius of the blend

From a purely gastronomic standpoint, pumpkin spice is a masterpiece of flavour chemistry. Warm cinnamon hits the front of your palate, ginger adds a tiny bite, nutmeg brings an earthy depth and cloves wrap the whole thing in rich nostalgia. It's the culinary equivalent of putting on your favourite oversized vintage sweater.

Toronto’s indie coffee shops and bakeries have long embraced the craft beyond corporate syrup pumps. We’re seeing house-made pumpkin spice syrups crafted with real grated ginger and whole cinnamon sticks, pumpkin-spiced cardamom buns, seasonal craft beers spiked with nutmeg and artisanal ice creams that elevate the blend into something genuinely sophisticated.

Let people have nice things

Food snobbery is boring, especially when it comes to seasonal comfort. We don’t shame people for obsessing over fiddleheads in the spring or fresh peaches in July, so why does autumn get hit with such intense cynicism?

Pumpkin spice isn't a crime. And liking it, or in my case, loving it, isn't either! It’s sweater weather, crisp autumn walks through Trinity Bellwoods and the return of cozy patio heaters.

So this fall, order the latte, grab the pumpkin-spiced donut from your local bakery and sip without a shred of shame.

Life is too short to drink plain espresso just to prove a point.

3 fall drinks to cozy up with this season

Whether you're hosting a dinner party or curling up on the couch to binge-watch your favourite show, these are the perfect fall drinks to pour this season.

Published: Tuesday 22nd September 2026

  • Booze

Brought to you by

Cozy season is here, the fall issue of foodism is out and we're filling up our fridges and bar carts with fall drinks that have depth. Now that wasps have firmly taken over the entire city and we're not running to Toronto's best patios every weekend, we can finally slow down, chew our food and savour every sip of our favourite fall drinks.

Inviting friends and family into our homes and trying out new recipes to serve finally feels like a joy again, instead of a chore. We're ready to use all of our favourite plateware, light candles and make our food and drink into a ritual. 

If you're not sure what to pour this season, we've rounded up the perfect fall drink for each category: wine lovers, cocktail makers and beer aficionados. Skip the guesswork for your next dinner party or cozy night in — these fall drinks will impress guests or simply elevate your evening. Happy fall. 

3 fall drinks to pour this season

1. Dillon's Fall Cocktail Kits

From $60

1 of 6

As much as we love our summer cocktails, it's time to retire them for the year and slip into something a little more suited to cooler days and longer nights. Luckily, you don't have to spend your own time researching different recipes, shopping for various ingredients and trialling cocktails before your guests arrive — or before you get a hankering for a delicious drink on a Thursday night.

Dillon's Small Batch Distillers has done all the work for you and prepared three new cocktail kits that come complete with all the ingredients you need. Just shake, strain and enjoy.

This season's serves include a herbal, fall take on the mule with black tea and bergamot syrup; a juicy gin fizz made with Provision's Fig & Vanilla Jam; and The Pancake Flip, a cocktail that combines the rich spice of Dillon's 100 per cent rye whisky with maple syrup. It's time to kiss chaotic summer goodbye and settle into the ease of fall — starting with these straightforward cocktail-making kits.

Shop now

2. 2023 Le Clos Jordanne Claystone Terrace Chardonnay

$37.19

Just because the leaves are turning red doesn't mean your wine has to. There are white wines that pair just as well with cozy sweaters, gorgeous fall tablescapes and comforting, hearty dishes, like the 2023 Le Clos Jordanne Claystone Terrace Chardonnay.

This serious Niagara chardonnay can both command the spotlight and play well with food. If you're feeling fancy and want to impress your guests, a chanterelle mushroom-stuffed chicken breast, lobster bisque, pan-seared scallops or cheese fondue will all shine next to this intense pour.

On an easy night, light your favourite candle, melt a brie wheel or make a mushroom risotto and watch even the everyday become elevated. With notes of white flowers, citrus, pineapple and guava, layered with rich toffee oak, Claystone won 96 points and Gold at Decanter.

Shop now

3. Beau's Barn Burner Amber Ale

$3.85

Crisp fall days with lingering rays of sunshine call for a toasty beer that can be easily enjoyed on a backyard patio, at the dinner table paired with a fall meal or by the fireplace as the evenings turn cold. Made in Ontario, Beau's Barn Burner is a light-bodied, easy-drinking amber ale that evolves a classic style into a more modern brew. 

Creamy, smooth and crushable, it marries a subtle malt sweetness with slightly bitter hops for just the right balance. On the nose, freshly baked biscuits, toffee and caramel will draw you in for more. This is one to savour sip by sip, whether you're pouring the brilliantly clear amber-hued beer solo or for a crowd.
